## 1. Question and hypotheses

Owner's question: feature-centric coordination, i.e.
- keeping orientation stable while an active contact is maintained, relative to another contact point or to the most
  recent contact point;
- decomposing target motion by contact sequences.

What exists today (the gap). The task contract already has typed outputs:
- `frame_estimate`, `contact_anchor` (pos, normal, validity, age, covariance), `alignment_receipt` and `completion_receipt`;
- `maintained_during` edges, with produced / consumed_by relations into the featurizer's interact bank.

So system i SEES anchors in its context. But the trained packet and its supervision are base-frame and uniform in time:
- knots are uniform (0.1 / 0.3 / 0.5 / 0.7 s);
- arm probes: rel_pos (position only), held_by, acting_on, observed_effect, subtask;
- legged probes: per-leg contact, goal, displacement, halt, turn;
- no orientation, contact-normal, tangent or wrench targets; touch is summarised to 3 scalars;
- system 0 gets no anchor-relative input;
- no metric of relative-frame drift, contact-sequence fidelity or re-anchoring;
- no learned model on the dual-arm tasks that need maintained contact (support_insert, handover; D-043).

Hypotheses (tested at EQUAL SUCCESS, section 5):
- **H1 (stability and sequencing).** A packet supervised with anchor-relative targets (pose of each hand and held
  object relative to the active contact anchor, contact phase and persistence per knot) gives lower maintained-contact
  orientation drift and higher contact-sequence fidelity than a base-frame packet with the same data, capacity, recipe
  and inputs. It also re-anchors (follows a shifted anchor) causally.
- **H2 (transfer).** The same anchor-relative packet transfers better to held-out bodies (arm family, attachment
  combination, ALOHA), because anchor-relative targets do not depend on the base frame or on link lengths.
- **Nulls reported as such:** no drift or fidelity difference beyond seed noise (failed_hypothesis), or a gain bought
  with a success loss larger than the non-inferiority margin.

### 2.1 Anchors
- An anchor is a contact_anchor receipt (or a frame_estimate). Anchor frame:
  - origin = anchor position;
  - z = the recorded normal;
  - x = the recorded tangent, else world +x projected on the tangent plane. In that case `yaw_defined = False`: the dual
    runtime records `tangent: None, tangent_yaw_uncertain: True`.
- Convention `runtime_v1` (the default for labels) reproduces the dual runtime's receipt: TCP position at the make
  tick, normal = −tool z. So labels and deploy-time inputs use the same frame.
- Convention `contact_point` uses the recorded mean contact point and normal instead.
- Roles per packet slot m:
  - **own**: m's most recent contact anchor. It stays active after release until m makes a new contact, like the
    runtime's anchor ("the most recent contact point").
  - **other**: the most recent LIVE contact of another manipulator. This is the maintained support (support_insert:
    the left hand on the fixture) or the partner's grasp (handover: the giver on the bar), i.e. "another contact point".
  - **frame**: the latest valid frame estimate (e.g. the located hole).
- Rotations use the 6D representation (first two columns, Gram–Schmidt inverse). Positions are in decimetres, as in
  the arm probes.

### 2.2 Contact segmentation and knots (label side)
- Per (a, b) pair, contact intervals are debounced: on after 2 ticks, off after 3 ticks (the analogue of the runtime's
  0.3 s anchor-loss debounce). Events: make, break, slide_start/end, pivot_start/end.
- Per-manipulator phase ∈ {free, approach, make, maintain, slide, pivot, release}:
  - slide = tangential TCP speed in the anchor frame above 2 cm/s;
  - pivot = relative rotation rate above 0.5 rad/s without sliding.
- Event-aligned knots (`knots-contact-v1`): make/break events inside the 0.8 s horizon take knots first, then
  slide/pivot events (keeping ≥ horizon/2K between knots). The remaining knots fill the uniform grid. With no events
  this equals the current uniform knots.
- The packet contract already carries `knot_times` per packet (`LatentActionChunk.knot_times`), so no contract change
  is needed. In variant A2, system i predicts the knot times (a small head supervised by the event times). The
  anchor-input system 0 accepts per-sample knot times [B, K].

### 2.3 Anchor-relative targets per knot (`anchor_relative_targets`)
For each knot k and slot m:
- `tcp_in_own`, `tcp_in_support` (the "other" anchor), `held_in_tcp` and `held_in_support`: 9-d poses (3-d position
  in dm plus 6D rotation), each with a mask;
- `normal_in_tcp`: live contact normal of m's own anchor pair, in the tool frame;
- `tangent_in_own`: direction of the tangential displacement since the make;
- `phase` [K, M];
- `contact_active` and `contact_persist` [K, C]: "which contacts persist over which knots", i.e. in contact
  continuously from the packet start through knot k.

Wrench is not a target in v1. It needs force sensing we do not model publicly (touch is a 3-scalar summary); listed as
future work, together with a public normal-force estimate.

### 2.4 Probes (`AnchorProbe`, bounded NLL)
- Query types: tcp_in_own, tcp_in_support, held_in_tcp, held_in_support (Gaussian over 9 dims), normal_in_tcp
  (loss 1 − cos), contact_phase (CE over 7 phases), contact_persist (BCE per knot × pair).
- Queries are addressed only by opaque fixed random codes (knot, packet slot, contact pair), with a metadata-only
  control, as for PacketProbe.
- The NLL is BOUNDED (D-085): log-variance floor −4, so NLL ≥ 0.5·d·(−4 + log 2π) (tested).
- `per_loss_grad_norms` logs every loss's gradient norm next to the clipped total. It is standard in every phase-C
  run, because D-085 was an unbounded loss starving system 0 through a shared clip.
- Metrics: position error (m), geodesic rotation error (deg), normal angle, phase and persistence accuracy.

### 2.5 System-0 input variant (`AnchorLatentRealizer`, `rz-anchor-in-v1`)
- It is LatentRealizer plus one input: per slot, 3 anchor blocks × 15 features. Each block holds valid, the TCP pose
  relative to the anchor (3 + 6), the anchor normal in the tool frame, log age and log covariance.
- The features are computed by `anchor_inputs(tcp_poses, receipts, …)` from FK of MEASURED joints
  (`DualSession.tcp_pose`) and the runtime's receipts only. The function takes no session, no truth and no object
  poses: it cannot read simulator ground truth (tested: shifting a receipt shifts the input by exactly −δ;
  invalid/other-type receipts are ignored).
- Each action node gets its own slot's features (`node_asm` routing, tested). The projection is zero-initialised, so
  `from_base(trained realizer)` starts bit-for-bit equal to the base (tested) and warm-starts a refit.

### 2.6 Privileged recorder
- `ContactFrameRecorder(session)` records, per control tick, TCP poses, object poses, per-pair contact flag, mean
  contact point, mean normal (from b into a) and held truth.
- It is read-only, works for DualSession and the arm Session, and serves LABELS and evaluation only.
- `collect_dual_episode(contact_labels=True)` (config key `contact_labels: true`) stores it in the PRIVATE episode file
  under `contact_frames`. The default is off, so episode files are unchanged.

## 3. Metrics (`rrp.evaluation.contact_metrics`, `cf_*` keys in the W6 `motion` field)
Keys are added only with `RRP_CONTACT_METRICS=1`, wired into:
- `legged_latent_eval.run_episode`: stance drift (`LeggedMotionRecorder(record_stance=True)`);
- `ladder` (arm): held-object drift vs the gripper;
- the dual audit, which always computes them.

The metrics:
- **Orientation drift of a held object relative to**
  - (a) the gripper: `cf_held_rot_drift_grip_max_rad`, plus the position drift `cf_held_pos_drift_grip_max_m`;
  - (b) the maintained support: the supporting hand's current TCP frame, over ticks where the object is held AND the
    other hand keeps a live contact (`cf_held_rot_drift_support_max_rad`, `_pos_`).
  Segments are debounced held intervals, trimmed by the make/release ticks; drift is measured from the segment start.
- **Support-anchor slip**: TCP displacement from the anchor over non-grasp maintained contacts
  (`cf_support_anchor_slip_max_m`).
- **Contact-sequence fidelity** vs the task spec, `TASK_CONTACT_SPECS` (from the task graphs):
  - precedence constraints; maintained constraints ("the support stays in contact until the peg is released";
    "the giver holds until the receiver grasps"); missing events;
  - `cf_contact_order_error` = violated / evaluable constraints;
  - vs a reference sequence (e.g. the teacher on the same seed): Kendall order error and timing MAE (s).
- **Re-anchoring latency**:
  - settle: time after a make until the TCP rests (< 1 cm/s for 5 ticks) in the new anchor frame;
  - receipt: time until the runtime publishes that actor's contact_anchor receipt.
- **Legged stance drift**: per debounced loaded stance, max horizontal displacement and |yaw change| of the foot from
  its touchdown pose (`cf_stance_pos_drift_*`, `cf_stance_yaw_drift_*`). Recorded from foot bodies and the loaded
  stance flag (normal force > 2% BW).
- All are unit-tested on synthetic trajectories with known answers (`tests/unit/test_contact_frames.py`).

## 4. Causal tests (`contact_edit_conditions` in `rrp.evaluation.edit_harness`)
Conditions and predictions are declared before any run:

| condition | kind | prediction |
|---|---|---|
| control / replay | control / replay | reference / noise floor |
| anchor_shift_context | semantic | shift the consumed anchor receipt by δ (3 cm, tangent plane): the dependent hand follows; projected shift gain in [0.5, 1.5], success kept |
| anchor_shift_packet | semantic | probe-guided z edit moving the dependent hand's anchor-relative readout by −δ (`anchor_shift_target`, other readouts anchored): same prediction |
| contact_order_swap | semantic | the other hand makes first contact: the observed sequence fits `swap_hands(spec)` better (order error vs the swapped spec drops) |
| anchor_shift_matched_random | irrelevant_control | a matched-norm random z direction: gain inside the replay noise floor |
| anchor_shift_probe_orthogonal | irrelevant_control | matched norm, orthogonal to the anchor-probe Jacobian: no re-anchoring |
| irrelevant_anchor_shift | irrelevant_control | the same δ on an anchor no active event consumes (a finished event's receipt, a released contact): no change |
| zero_packet | negative_control | z = 0 |

Statistics: `paired_effect` with `projected_shift(key, δ)`: paired bootstrap CI and sign-flip permutation p over
paired seeds, per training seed, then pooled (the D-090 protocol). The re-anchoring gain is mean_diff / |δ|.

Where the context edit acts: base-frame and anchor packets both see the receipt in system i's context (same input
information). The anchor-input system 0 additionally sees it directly, so the variant A3-ctrl (below) separates
"system 0 reads the anchor" from "the packet encodes the anchor".

## 5. Experiments (phases B–D)

### Phase B: dual-arm data fit for purpose (peer CPU)
1. Audit the scripted dual teachers under current physics (done in phase A, section 7).
2. Teacher v3 (`rrp.teachers.dual`, new version; v2 stays the default):
   - minimum-jerk submovements as in W7's arm_smooth v2;
   - the grasp and hand-off fixes the audit calls for;
   - contact-safe DART: phase-gated noise (free-space phases only) as in D-121, never during l_hold, r_align, r_insert,
     offer, receive or release.
   Gate on the W6/D-112 arm criteria per arm, plus a new maintained-contact criterion: support-anchor slip and held
   drift at the teacher's own level.
3. Collect `support_insert_v3` and `handover_v3` with `contact_labels: true` and grasp_v2.1. Same source pairs and
   seeds as the v2 datasets; clean plus phase-gated DART; dev and held-out pairs as in the split files.
4. Pack with the anchor targets as extra per-row arrays; knot ticks are stored for both uniform and event-aligned knots.

### Phase C: training matrix on dual data (peer GPU); same data, steps, capacity and recipe (semfix, bounded NLL)
- **B0** base-frame packet: the current dual recipe (per-slot rel_pos, held_by, acting_on, subtask) with an AnchorProbe
  trained on DETACHED z (diagnostic readout only), so both arms carry the same parameters and readouts.
- **A1** = B0 + anchor-relative supervision on the packet (AnchorProbe loss into E); uniform knots; base system 0.
- **A2** = A1 + event-aligned knots (system i predicts knot times).
- **A3** = A1 + anchor-input system 0 (`rz-anchor-in-v1`).
- **A3-ctrl** = B0 + anchor-input system 0 (separates the input from the packet).
- nosem reference (as D-095).
Fairness:
- identical packed data and input information (receipts are in every variant's context);
- identical Stage A / flow / refit step counts and batch sizes;
- the total semantic loss weight is held equal (the anchor loss replaces part of rel_pos' weight in A*);
- the zero-initialised anchor projection's extra ~8.6k parameters in A3/A3-ctrl are matched in B0/A1 by the same layer
  fed zeros;
- per-loss gradient norms are logged in every run;
- 3 training seeds; fresh eval seed sets.

### Phase D: evaluation and transfer
- Closed loop on dev seeds: success (privileged), W6 motion gates, `cf_*` metrics.
- Causal suite of section 4 on 40 paired seeds per model.
- H2: budget-0 transfer to held-out pairs:
  - xarm7_pg2__xarm7_pg2 and xarm7_tf3__xarm7_pg2 (arm family);
  - parm6_pg2__panda_tf3 and ur5e_pg2__panda_tf3 (attachment combination);
  - aloha (dual-arm body).
  Then small-budget adaptation (system 0 only), reported separately from the transfer of the existing controller.
- Legged H1 analogue: re-evaluate the existing W8 anymal_c / go2 semfix vs nosem checkpoints with
  `RRP_CONTACT_METRICS=1` on the D-090 seeds (stance drift). No training.

Decision rules:
- **Equal success**: a success difference inside ±5 points (paired, 95% CI) on dev.
- **H1 supported** if, at equal success, A1 or A3 lowers `cf_held_rot_drift_support_max_rad` and
  `cf_contact_order_error` against B0 in every training seed (pooled paired CI excludes 0), AND anchor_shift_* gains
  exceed their matched controls.
- **H2 supported** if the held-out success or drift gap favours A* in ≥ 2/3 target pairs with a pooled CI excluding 0.
Otherwise the result is failed_hypothesis, with the numbers.

## 6. Compute estimates (measured basis in brackets)
- **Phase B collection.**
  - Teacher episode 13 s (uncontended smoke) to 27 s (contended audit) per 1200 control steps on one peer CPU.
    Collection also featurizes, so assume ~40 s.
  - support_insert: 8 source pairs × 100 seeds × (clean + phase-gated DART) = 1,600 episodes, plus dev and held-out
    ≈ 1,900. Handover: 6 × 100 × 2 = 1,200 plus dev ≈ 1,400.
  - Total ≈ 3,300 episodes × 40 s ≈ 37 CPU-h ⇒ about 4.5 h on 8 peer CPUs.
  - Pack about 1 h. Pack size ~15–20 GB: the v1 dual pack was 14 GB for 525k rows, plus ~2 KB per row for anchor
    targets. The peer disk and RAM store must be checked first (peer disk was 97% full).
- **Teacher v3 development.** About 1 day of agent time, mostly on the host (editing plus tiny smokes on the peer).
- **Phase C.**
  - Dual Stage A ran at ~2 s/step on the contended GPU (dualarm track). Assume 0.6–2 s/step: 8k steps ≈ 1.3–4.4 GPU-h;
    flow 8k ≈ 1–3 GPU-h; system-0 refit ≈ 0.5–1 GPU-h. So ≈ 3–8 GPU-h per variant-seed.
  - Full matrix (5 variants + nosem) × 3 seeds = 18 runs ≈ 55–145 GPU-h.
  - Staged: C1 = B0, A1, A3 × seed 0 (≈ 9–24 GPU-h). Go/no-go on dev drift and success. Then C2 = seeds 1–2 and
    A2/A3-ctrl only if C1 is promising.
  - Memory per Stage A run: declare ≥ 1.35 × the measured peak (D-117). The v1 chain used 32G + 16G GPU.
- **Phase D.**
  - Closed-loop dual episode ≈ 30–40 s CPU.
  - Per model: 60 dev + 40 seeds × 9 edit conditions ≈ 420 episodes ≈ 4.5 CPU-h. 18 models ≈ 80 CPU-h ≈ 10 h on
    8 CPUs; C1 only ≈ 13 CPU-h.
  - Transfer: 5 targets × 60 seeds × 6 models ≈ 20 CPU-h.
  - Legged re-evals: ≈ 1 CPU-h per checkpoint × 12 checkpoints.
- **Total C1 path (go/no-go)**: ≈ 40 CPU-h + 9–24 GPU-h. **Full path**: ≈ 200 CPU-h + 55–145 GPU-h.
